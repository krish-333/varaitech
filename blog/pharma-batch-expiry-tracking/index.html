Batch and Expiry Tracking for Pharma Distributors and Chemists

HealthcareBy VARAI Technologies2 Nov 20263 min read

For a pharma distributor or chemist, expired stock is a direct loss, and knowing which batch went to which customer is a compliance need. Doing this from bills and registers is slow and easy to get wrong.

These are the main places manual tracking breaks down.

1. Near-expiry stock is found too late

If expiry dates are checked by hand during a stock walk, items are discovered close to or past their date. By then the chance to sell, discount or return them is gone.

2. Supplier return windows are missed

Many suppliers accept returns only within a window. Without a reminder, that deadline passes unnoticed and the loss lands on you.

3. Batch tracing takes hours

When a query or recall comes in, finding every customer who received a batch means searching through bills. It is stressful when time matters.

4. Stock and margin are guesses

Batch-wise purchase rates, margins and fast and slow movers are hard to see from paper and sheets, so reordering relies on habit.

What a better system looks like

Batch and expiry are captured at purchase and carried through to every sale. Near-expiry lists appear on a schedule with a prompt to sell, discount or return in time. Batch tracing becomes a search, and stock and margin reports are always current.

It sits beside your billing and accounts rather than replacing them, so your team does not have to relearn everything.

What to do about it

Start with your highest-value and shortest-shelf-life products. Once expiry alerts are saving stock, extend to the rest of the range.
