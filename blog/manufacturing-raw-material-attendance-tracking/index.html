Raw Material, Scrap and Shift Attendance: Moving a Small Factory Off Paper

ManufacturingBy VARAI Technologies2 Nov 20263 min read

In a small engineering or fabrication unit, the owner is often also the stores manager, the planner and the supervisor. Paper registers keep production moving, but they hide where material and time are being lost.

Here are the four places we see the most leakage in small factories that still run on paper.

1. Raw material is bought on feel

Without a clear record of what came in, what was issued to which job and what is left, purchases are made on memory. The result is either over-purchase that ties up cash, or a stock-out that stops a job.

2. Scrap and wastage go unnoticed

Offcuts, rejects and rework are rarely recorded job by job, so nobody knows which jobs or machines waste the most. What is not measured is hard to improve.

3. Attendance and overtime end in disputes

Shift attendance collected on paper or by memory is argued about at payroll time. Overtime is hard to prove, and supervisors spend hours preparing numbers.

4. Purchase orders run on phone calls

Which supplier owes what by which date is often known by one person. Late deliveries are found only when the machine is idle.

What a better system looks like

Material is recorded in, issued to a job and left as scrap in a few taps on the shop floor. Attendance is captured by shift, with overtime and payroll-ready summaries. Open purchase orders show which jobs are waiting on material.

It has to be simple enough for supervisors and operators, so it is built around your shifts, your jobs and your language, not a generic template.

What to do about it

Pick the loss that hurts most, often raw material or attendance, and fix that first. Once the team sees the time saved, adding the rest is easy.
